## Core Rule

**Contract value is not profit. Customer deposits are not profit. Available credit is not company capital unless formally contributed/loaned and documented.**

## Job Economics Definitions

- **Contract revenue:** agreed value of authorized work, including approved change orders.
- **Collected revenue:** cash actually received.
- **Deposit/progress payment:** customer funds received under contract terms; not automatically earned profit.
- **Materials:** project-specific material costs.
- **Direct labor:** labor directly attributable to the project, including properly documented owner labor if an adopted compensation method exists.
- **Subcontractors:** third-party trade/project costs.
- **Disposal:** dumpsters, haul-off, dump fees, cleanup disposal.
- **Permits/fees:** project-specific jurisdictional costs.
- **Rentals:** project-specific equipment/tool rentals.
- **Transportation/delivery:** project-attributable logistics where tracked.
- **Contingency:** planning allowance for uncertainty; not a substitute for disciplined scoping.
- **Direct job cost:** total project-attributable costs.
- **Gross job profit:** contract/earned revenue less direct job costs under the adopted accounting method.
- **Gross margin:** gross job profit divided by applicable revenue.
- **Company overhead:** non-project-specific operating expense.
- **Operating profit:** revenue less direct costs and company overhead, before applicable taxes/other items.
- **Tax reserve:** cash set aside for anticipated tax obligations under professional guidance.
- **Warranty/rework reserve:** optional later-adopted reserve based on actual risk/history.
- **Cash reserve:** retained liquidity for operations and risk.
- **Owner labor compensation:** payment for actual services under an adopted compensation system; separate from ownership distribution.
- **Owner distribution:** payment based on ownership/economic rights from legitimately distributable funds.
- **Retained earnings/capital:** value intentionally retained in the company rather than distributed.

## Preliminary Job-Cost Logic

For every estimate:

1. Define scope and exclusions.
2. Quantify materials using current supplier evidence where practical.
3. Estimate labor hours/production days.
4. Identify subcontracted/regulated work.
5. Add disposal, permits, rentals, transport/delivery, and other job costs.
6. Identify uncertainty/contingency.
7. Apply an adopted overhead/profit methodology once evidence exists.
8. Stress-test schedule and scope assumptions.
9. Record the estimate baseline.
10. Compare actuals to estimate after completion.

Build Room 00 does not invent market pricing, hourly rates, markup percentages, or target margins.

## Cash-Control Principles

- Separate company and personal funds after banking is legally established.
- Record every inflow/outflow with business purpose.
- Customer payments should map to a job/project identifier.
- Do not use one customer’s funds to conceal another project’s loss.
- Maintain visibility into committed materials/subcontractor obligations before distributions.
- Establish tax and operating reserves before routine owner distributions.
- Related-party payments must be documented.

## Owner Distribution Gate

No automatic 70/30 distribution occurs merely because cash enters the bank. Distribution requires reconciliation of earned revenue, direct costs, overhead, obligations, reserves, taxes, owner compensation, and cash needs under later adopted policy.

## Purpose

Establish Shell & Co Remodeling as a lean residential remodeling company that begins with bathroom remodeling and converts founder skill, existing tools, business infrastructure, and customer acquisition into profitable completed work and documented operating history.

## Market Problem

Homeowners need reliable residential remodeling execution, clear scopes, workmanship, communication, and project accountability. Build Room 00 treats this as a working market thesis; local demand, competitive intensity, pricing, and customer-acquisition economics remain to be validated with evidence before being treated as quantified facts.

## Target Customer

Initial target customer: homeowners in Southern Indiana and the broader Kentuckiana market seeking bathroom renovation, shower/tile, flooring, fixture, and related interior remodeling work, subject to jurisdictional authorization and project fit.

## Initial Service Wedge

Bathroom remodeling is the controlled market-entry wedge because founder-supplied capability and project evidence exist in this domain. The company is not canonically limited to bathrooms.

## Value Proposition

Combine technical craftsmanship and field judgment with disciplined business systems: clear intake, scopes, estimates, documented changes, job costing, quality control, and customer follow-through.

## Founder Complementarity

**Bernard Shell Jr.:** technical knowledge, craftsmanship, tools/equipment, remodeling experience, field execution, workmanship standards.  
**Elijah L. Cooley:** business architecture, administration, digital systems, AI/automation, customer acquisition, documentation, fundability, operations development.

## Operating Model

Lead → qualification → site visit → scope → estimate → contract → approved payment trigger → scheduling → procurement → production → change control → QC → completion → collection → review/referral → archive.

Initial delivery should remain lean: founders plus qualified project-specific labor/subcontractors as legally and operationally appropriate.

## Revenue Model

Primary revenue: contracted residential remodeling projects and authorized change orders. Revenue is recognized operationally only as actually earned/collected under the applicable accounting method; deposits and contract values are not profit.

## Cost Model

Direct costs may include materials, owner/direct labor, subcontractors, disposal, permits, rentals, transportation, project-specific expenses, contingency, and warranty/rework exposure. Company overhead must be tracked separately.

## Capital Philosophy

**Principle:** Do not spend capital merely because capital or credit is available.

Priority order:
1. Legality/compliance readiness
2. Insurance/risk control
3. Customer acquisition
4. Materials and productive job needs
5. Completion quality
6. Revenue collection
7. Reserves
8. Productive expansion

Avoid premature offices, vehicles, unnecessary equipment, excessive payroll, oversized advertising commitments, and unnecessary software.

## First-Job Strategy

Goal: complete one profitable, well-documented, permission-based portfolio-worthy project under the company’s authorized operating structure.

Required proof system, subject to customer permission:
- before photos
- work-in-progress photos
- after photos
- scope summary
- material/craftsmanship notes
- testimonial/review request
- referral request
- project case-study inputs

No fabricated project imagery, testimonials, customer identities, or reviews.

## Proof-Building Strategy

Each completed project should create reusable, truthful evidence for the next sales cycle: portfolio assets, reviews, referral opportunities, cost/margin data, schedule data, and process lessons.

## Growth Stages

**Stage I — Founder-led craft operation:** founders and project-specific resources.  
**Stage II — Managed remodeling operation:** repeatable workflows, qualified labor bench, stronger subcontractor network, predictable lead flow.  
**Stage III — Scalable contractor platform:** multiple crews/project management only after legal, financial, quality, and demand evidence supports expansion.

## Carfixd Separation

Carfixd Automotive is legally/financially separate in this architecture. No commingling, silent asset transfer, customer-fund sharing, or implied shared liability is authorized.

## Known Risks

- unresolved 30% ownership vehicle
- legal/name availability and formation requirements
- jurisdiction-specific contractor licensing/registration/permits
- insurance requirements
- scope creep/change-order failure
- underpricing and poor job costing
- labor/subcontractor quality
- cash-flow mismatch between deposits, procurement, payroll, and collections
- callback/rework exposure
- customer-acquisition cost uncertainty
- founder role/authority ambiguity if governance is not formalized

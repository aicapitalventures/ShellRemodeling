## Controlled Pipeline

**Lead → Qualification → Customer Intake → Site Visit → Scope Development → Estimate → Follow-Up → Contract → Deposit / Approved Payment Trigger → Scheduling → Material Selection → Procurement → Pre-Start Verification → Production → Daily Documentation → Change Orders → Quality Control → Punch List → Final Invoice → Completion → Customer Acceptance → Review Request → Referral Request → Project Archive**

## Stage Controls

### 1. Lead / Qualification
Capture source, contact route, general project type, property location, timeline, budget context when appropriate, and whether the request fits authorized service scope.

**Decision:** pursue / nurture / decline / refer.

### 2. Customer Intake
Capture project facts without promising price, schedule, permits, or regulated work before inspection and verification.

### 3. Site Visit
Document conditions, measurements, access, visible risks, customer priorities, likely material selections, demolition implications, and potential regulated-trade dependencies.

### 4. Scope Development
Translate the visit into explicit inclusions/exclusions, allowances, assumptions, owner responsibilities, subcontractor dependencies, change-order triggers, and payment logic.

### 5. Estimate
Estimate must distinguish scope, price, assumptions, exclusions, expiration/validity where appropriate, and conditions. Pricing must be supported by job-cost logic rather than intuition alone.

### 6. Follow-Up
Track customer questions and revisions. No verbal scope revision becomes binding merely because it was discussed.

### 7. Contract
No production starts without the legally required/approved contract documentation for that project class and jurisdiction.

### 8. Deposit / Approved Payment Trigger
Customer funds are not profit. Deposit/progress-payment handling must follow lawful contract terms and company cash controls.

### 9. Scheduling / Procurement
Schedule only after prerequisites are satisfied: contract, payment trigger, permits/approvals where required, material availability, labor/subcontractor availability, and access.

### 10. Pre-Start Verification
Confirm scope, selections, permits, trade assignments, material readiness, customer access, site protection, disposal, and schedule assumptions.

### 11. Production / Daily Documentation
Maintain project notes sufficient to record progress, material issues, delays, customer decisions, photos where authorized, labor/subcontractor activity, and risks.

### 12. Change Orders
No material scope expansion should proceed without documented approval and price/schedule impact except genuine urgent safety conditions handled under an adopted emergency protocol.

### 13. Quality Control / Punch List
Technical authority verifies workmanship against scope, manufacturer requirements where relevant, applicable code/permit requirements, and company quality standards.

### 14. Final Invoice / Completion
Reconcile approved changes, remaining balance, punch-list completion, and customer acceptance documentation.

### 15. Review / Referral / Archive
Ask for truthful reviews and referrals without coercion. Archive non-sensitive project metadata publicly only if authorized; private customer/project files belong in protected storage, not this repository.

## Evidence Requirements

At each stage, the company should be able to show what decision was made, who made it, what evidence supported it, and what changed. Critical evidence includes estimates, scopes, signed approvals, payment records, change orders, job-cost records, QC/punch records, and completion status. Sensitive records must remain private.

## Prohibited Shortcuts

- starting regulated work without required authorization
- starting material work before applicable contract/payment prerequisites
- treating verbal additions as free work
- undocumented change orders
- spending customer funds without job/cash-control visibility
- closing a project with unresolved known punch items unless expressly documented
- fabricating reviews or project evidence
